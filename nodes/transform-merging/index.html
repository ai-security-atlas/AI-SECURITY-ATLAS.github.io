Model and adapter merging

Combine compatible parameters or updates; provenance and behavioral regressions must be reassessed.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Model and adapter merging combines compatible parameters or updates from more than one source into a single model. This can mean averaging or otherwise fusing the weights of several fine-tuned models, or folding one or more adapters into a base, to blend their capabilities without a fresh training run. Techniques for resolving conflicts between the merged updates are discussed in TIES-Merging: Resolving Interference When Merging Models.

Merging operates on a model’s weights, parameters and activations, which must be compatible in architecture and layout for the combination to be meaningful. As its summary stresses, provenance and behavioral regressions have to be reassessed after a merge, because the combined model may behave differently from any of its parts, including on safety-relevant behavior.

The technique is neutral and widely used to consolidate capabilities or reuse community adapters. It is relevant to security because it can be used by behavioral tampering through merges or conversion, where merging is a vector for slipping altered behavior into an otherwise trusted model. This is why the origin of each merged component, and testing of the result, matter. Specific merge algorithms and their interference-resolution strategies are left to the cited source.
