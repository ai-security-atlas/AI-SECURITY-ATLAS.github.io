Data collection, filtering and deduplication

Dataset lineage and preprocessing affect privacy, integrity and evaluation reliability.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Data collection, filtering and deduplication is the foundational lifecycle stage where raw corpora are gathered, cleaned, filtered and deduplicated before any training objective is applied. Its preprocessing choices — what is included, how duplicates are removed, and how sources are documented — shape a model’s later privacy, integrity and evaluation reliability. Because no earlier stage feeds it, the trust placed in data sources here propagates forward: pretraining and downstream evaluation both depend on it.

Security-relevant scope

Several concerns are relevant to this stage rather than proven at it. Dataset lineage bears on whether later training-data minimization and deduplication and dataset and artifact provenance practices can be applied. It is also the point at which data-centric manipulation such as pretraining data poisoning or trigger-conditioned model backdoors would, if present, be introduced. Deduplication additionally affects how memorization and extraction risk are later analyzed.

Keeping training data cleanly separated from evaluation material is also what makes evaluation and benchmark governance meaningful. The cited work on extracting training data and the NIST adversarial-ML taxonomy give empirical and definitional grounding for these exposures; treat the attack references as scope, not as demonstrated compromise of any particular dataset.
