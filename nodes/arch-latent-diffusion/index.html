Latent diffusion pipelines

Diffusion operates in a learned latent space, with separate encoding and decoding components.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Latent diffusion pipelines run the diffusion generative model process not on raw pixels but in a learned, lower-dimensional latent space. A variational autoencoder encodes inputs into that latent space and decodes samples back out, while diffusion denoises within it. Moving the expensive iterative denoising into a compact latent representation reduces compute while preserving output quality. The atlas records both dependencies: the pipeline requires diffusion and a VAE, and it uses the VAE as its encoding and decoding component.

Why it matters for security analysis

The defining feature for analysis is that a latent diffusion pipeline is a composite of separable parts — an encoder, a latent-space denoiser, and a decoder — each of which is its own surface. A property of one component does not automatically characterize the whole, and concerns should be attributed to the specific stage they touch. As with diffusion generally, the process is denoising-based rather than autoregressive, so language-generation techniques should not be assumed to apply. This node carries no relevant-technique edges of its own; applicable scope is best inherited from the underlying diffusion generative models and from the variational autoencoders it embeds, evaluated against the concrete pipeline. Empirical details are deferred to the cited literature.
