Preference or reward-signal manipulation targets the comparisons, reward models and human-feedback signals used during post-training. The objective is to manipulate outputs, predictions or decisions or to violate intended behavioral constraints by shaping what the alignment process learns to reward.

How it works

Alignment pipelines depend on preference data and reward models: pairs or rankings of candidate responses that teach a model which behaviors are favored. If an attacker can influence those comparisons — by contributing biased preferences, corrupting a reward model, or steering feedback — the learned reward signal can be nudged toward attacker-preferred behavior. The attacker controls contributed feedback; the victim controls collection, filtering and the training procedure, so influence is contingent on how much of the signal an attacker can reach and how it is weighted.

This is a coverage node grounded in threat modeling for decoder-only language models; it is not a claim that any specific preference-optimization method is inherently vulnerable. It sits within the training-and-adaptation abuse family, distinct from poisoning that targets pretraining or supervised demonstrations because it acts on the reward channel itself.

Evaluation is framed through an explicit threat model and attack success and severity. For the underlying feedback and preference-optimization methods, and for taxonomy framing, defer to the cited sources rather than assuming present-day exploitability.
