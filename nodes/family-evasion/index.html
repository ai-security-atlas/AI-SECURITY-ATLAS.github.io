Adversarial input evasion

Perturb inputs or their presentation to change predictions or guardrail decisions.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

This family gathers techniques that perturb inputs, or how inputs are presented, to change a model’s prediction or a guardrail’s decision. The unifying assumption is that small, often human-imperceptible changes to an input can push a model across a decision boundary, whether the target is a classifier, a detector, or a safety filter.

Members range from classic optimization methods to physical and multimodal variants. Gradient-based adversarial perturbations / FGSM and Iterative projected adversarial optimization compute perturbations against model gradients, single-step and iterative respectively. Evasion of an ML security detector applies the same idea to defensive classifiers. Physical-world adversarial examples are meant to survive capture through a camera or sensor, while Optimized visual jailbreaks craft images that relax a multimodal model’s behavioral constraints.

These route toward Evade security controls or accountability, Manipulate outputs, predictions or decisions, and—for visual jailbreaks—Violate intended behavioral constraints. The line between this family and prompt-and-context manipulation can blur where images carry instructions; membership here emphasizes perturbation of the input signal over injected natural-language instructions. Use the grouping to navigate the adversarial-example literature and its extensions to modern multimodal and safety settings.
