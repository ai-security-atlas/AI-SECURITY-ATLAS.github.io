Knowledge distillation

Teacher-student transfer can be authorized development or support unauthorized functional imitation.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Knowledge distillation transfers behavior from a larger or more capable teacher model into a smaller or cheaper student. Rather than training the student only on hard labels, the method trains it to match the teacher’s outputs, such as soft probability distributions, so the student absorbs some of the teacher’s learned structure. The original framing appears in Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network.

As a training procedure, distillation depends on supervised fine-tuning: the student is optimized on teacher-provided targets over a dataset. This makes it a standard tool for model compression, latency reduction and deploying capable models on constrained hardware.

The same mechanism has a dual character noted in its summary. Authorized teacher-student transfer is ordinary development, but the technique can also support unauthorized functional imitation of a model an actor does not own. It is relevant to black-box functional model extraction, which reconstructs behavior through query access, and can be used by unauthorized teacher-student capability transfer. The distinction turns on authorization and provenance rather than on the algorithm itself, which remains neutral. Empirical trade-offs between compression and retained capability are deferred to the cited source.
