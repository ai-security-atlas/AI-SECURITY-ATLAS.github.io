Token, context and inference-budget exhaustion

Separate bounded expensive requests from sustained denial of service and per-tenant quota failure.

Cause excessive permitted work through input/output length or uncontrolled request behavior.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Token, context and inference-budget exhaustion drives a system to perform excessive permitted work through long inputs or outputs, or through uncontrolled request behavior. It maps to the OWASP LLM Top 10 unbounded-consumption category. The objective is to degrade availability or increase cost.

How it works

The technique exploits the resource cost of processing large context windows and generating long responses on inference engines and serving gateways. By supplying maximal-length inputs, eliciting long generations, or issuing requests without restraint, an attacker can consume a disproportionate share of compute, tokens or throughput. The attacker controls request size and rate; the victim controls the limits, quotas and scheduling that decide how much work is actually permitted. Impact is therefore contingent on what bounds the operator has in place.

A caveat sharpens the framing: bounded but expensive individual requests should be separated from sustained denial of service and from per-tenant quota failures — they represent different severities. The technique belongs to the availability-and-cost family and complements sponge inputs, which pursue pathological cost rather than sheer length.

A candidate mitigation, applied conditionally, is enforcing request, token, tool and cost budgets. Evaluation is framed through an explicit threat model, attack success and severity, and query, token, latency and compute cost measures. Defer to the cited OWASP source for scope.
