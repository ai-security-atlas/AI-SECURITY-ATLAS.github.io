Claim strength and verification depth

Separate theory, empirical reporting, independent reproduction, deployment reports and editorial hypotheses.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Claim strength and verification depth is about grading how well a stated result is actually supported. Not every claim in the literature rests on the same footing, and this measure separates the levels: a theoretical argument, a single empirical demonstration, an independent reproduction, a report from real deployment, and an editorial hypothesis are distinct kinds of evidence that should not be treated interchangeably. Attaching the right label to a claim keeps a knowledge base honest about what is known versus what is conjectured.

The practical value is calibration. A finding backed only by one paper’s own experiments warrants more caution than one others have reproduced; a deployment report speaks to real conditions but may lack controlled comparison; an editorial hypothesis flags reasoning that has not yet been tested at all. Reference taxonomies and curated attack knowledge bases exist partly to impose this kind of structure on scattered results.

A common pitfall is citation laundering, where a tentative claim hardens into accepted fact through repeated secondhand citation. This measure is scope-limited: labeling evidence strength does not itself verify a claim, and a well-cited result can still be wrong or context-dependent. Strong verification depth for one setting is not proof the effect generalizes or that a system is safe. Consult the cited taxonomy and knowledge-base sources for how attack and mitigation claims are categorized and what verification they carry.
