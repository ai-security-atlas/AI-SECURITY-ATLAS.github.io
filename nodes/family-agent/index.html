Agent authority and workflow attacks

Redirect tool use, memory or inter-agent workflows across trust boundaries.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

This family groups techniques that abuse the trust an agentic system places in its tools, memory, and collaborating agents. What unifies them is a shared assumption: once a model is granted authority to act—calling tools, reading persistent state, or exchanging messages with other agents—an attacker who can influence any of those channels may redirect that authority across a trust boundary. No model compromise is required; the model can behave as designed while acting on manipulated context.

Members span the tool interface and the workflow around it. Tool-description poisoning and Tool-output injection plant instructions in the metadata or results a tool returns, while Tool definition or behavior changes after approval exploit the gap between review and execution. Agent or connector confused-deputy abuse and Exploitation of excessive delegated agency turn over-broad permissions into unauthorized actions. Improper handling of model-generated output, Inter-agent trust and message manipulation, and Persistent agent-memory poisoning extend the surface to output sinks, multi-agent messaging, and long-lived state.

Collectively these route toward objectives such as Cause unauthorized actions or privilege use, Obtain protected information, Establish persistent attacker influence, and Compromise the surrounding execution environment. Treat the grouping as an orientation aid: several members overlap with the prompt-injection and poisoning families and belong here because the payoff is control over agent authority.
