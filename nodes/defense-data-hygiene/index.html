Training-data minimization and deduplication

Reduce unnecessary sensitive or repeated records; does not provide a formal privacy guarantee.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Training-data minimization and deduplication reduce how much unnecessary sensitive information, and how many repeated records, enter a model’s training set. It operates during data collection, filtering and deduplication: excluding data that is not needed, and collapsing duplicates so that individual records are not seen many times. The rationale is that memorization — and thus later extraction — is strongly associated with data that is either sensitive-by-inclusion or repeated often enough to be reproduced.

As a mitigation it is conditional and offers no formal guarantee. It can lower the risk of language-model training-data extraction and diffusion training-image extraction by shrinking the pool of memorizable sensitive content and reducing duplication, which cited work links to memorization. Residual risk remains: unique but sensitive records can still be memorized, deduplication is imperfect against near-duplicates and paraphrases, and minimization trades away data that may carry utility. It bounds exposure rather than eliminating it.

For a stated, quantifiable privacy property, this control is distinct from differentially private training, which provides accounting that data hygiene does not. Refer to the cited study on extracting training data for the memorization dynamics involved.
