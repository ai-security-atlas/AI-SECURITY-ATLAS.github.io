Physical-world adversarial examples

Digital success does not imply physical success; transformations are part of the threat model.

Study perturbations after printing, capture or other real-world transformations.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Physical-world adversarial examples study whether an adversarial input still fools a model after it passes through real-world transformations — printing, photographing, changes in lighting, angle or distance. The objective is to evade security controls or to manipulate outputs, predictions or decisions, for instance in perception systems built on convolutional networks.

How it works

The starting point is a digital adversarial perturbation produced with gradient-based methods. Moving that perturbation into the physical world introduces distortions the attacker does not fully control: a printed pattern is re-sampled by a camera, colors shift, and geometry warps. The technique therefore treats these transformations as part of the threat model, seeking perturbations that survive them rather than assuming digital success carries over. A key caveat follows directly — success against a stored image does not imply success once the same perturbation is printed and re-captured.

Conceptually, exposure depends on the capture pipeline and the physical conditions an attacker can influence, which vary widely across deployments. The technique is a member of the adversarial input-evasion family and shares its optimization roots with digital-only variants while adding a robustness-to-transformation requirement.

Evaluation is framed through adaptive defense evaluation, an explicit threat model, and attack success and severity. For the experimental conditions under which physical transfer was studied, defer to the cited source.
