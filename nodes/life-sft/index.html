Supervised fine-tuning

Optimize on labeled demonstrations or input-output examples.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is a post-training stage that optimizes a model on labeled demonstrations or input–output examples, teaching it to produce desired responses for given inputs. It requires post-training as its context and moves a base model toward specific behaviors by imitation of curated examples.

SFT underpins a wide range of later work. Safety and instruction tuning builds on it to instill constraints, and adaptation methods including full-parameter fine-tuning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, knowledge distillation and DreamBooth personalization all use this labeled-example paradigm.

Security-relevant scope

Because SFT changes a model’s behavior from data, the sourcing of its demonstrations matters. Instruction-tuning data poisoning and adversarial fine-tuning to alter constraints are the manipulation scenarios relevant to this stage — concerns about who supplies the examples and objective, not demonstrated properties of any particular run. Fine-tuning can also affect behaviors established earlier, so properties present before SFT may warrant re-checking afterward. The cited instruction-following work and the study of fine-tuning’s effect on safety describe these dynamics; consult them for empirical results.
