Text, image and control conditioning

Conditioning can enter through text encoders, cross-attention or additional control branches.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Conditioning is how a generative model is steered toward a particular output rather than sampling freely. In text-to-image systems, for example, the conditioning is the prompt (and any auxiliary control signal) that tells the model what to generate. Crucially, conditioning can enter through several distinct pathways: a text encoder that turns a prompt into vectors, cross-attention layers that inject those vectors into the generation process, or additional control branches that carry structural signals such as edges or poses.

Because it builds on attention, conditioning inherits attention’s role as an injection point — each pathway is a separate surface where external information shapes the result. Recognizing that there is more than one such surface is the practical takeaway: text, spatial control, and other modalities may each reach the model through their own route.

This concept is a prerequisite for classifier-free guidance, which amplifies how strongly the output follows the conditioning, and for ControlNet-style conditioning, which adds dedicated control branches.

For a security engineer, conditioning surfaces are where the “instructions” to a generative model live. Whether a given surface can be manipulated, and to what effect, depends on the specific system and is treated by the techniques that build on this concept; the concept itself just maps where those inputs enter.
