Transfer and architecture boundaries

Distinguish source-model success, target-model transfer, adaptation and unsupported generalization.

AI-generated draft · conceptual overview, not yet reviewed

Transfer and architecture boundaries concerns being precise about where an attack actually works. A result achieved against the model it was optimized on (source-model success) is different from the same attack landing on a model it was never tuned against (target-model transfer), which is different again from a version adapted to the new target, and different still from an unsupported leap of generalization that the evidence does not back. Blurring these lets a narrow demonstration read as a universal one.

The distinction matters most for optimized attacks such as adversarial suffixes and visual jailbreaks, where the crafting process is tied to a specific model and architecture. Some of these attacks are studied precisely because they transfer across models, but transfer is a property to be measured on named targets, not assumed. Cross-modal and cross-architecture claims especially require direct evidence rather than extrapolation.

A common pitfall is reporting source-model success and implying broad portability, or citing transfer to a few models as if it covered a whole architecture class. Transfer results are scope-limited: they hold for the specific source and target models tested, and success on those is not proof that an attack works on other architectures, later revisions, or a given deployed system. Where an attack requires adaptation to transfer, that adaptation cost belongs in the report. Defer to the cited sources for which source and target models were tested and the transfer rates observed.
