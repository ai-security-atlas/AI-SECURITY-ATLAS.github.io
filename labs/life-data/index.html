Lab guide

Atlas node: Data collection, filtering and deduplication · Family: family-poisoning · Level: intermediate · Time: 75 min · Model backend: none

Learning path: Module 10 · step 2 of 6 · Data, evaluation integrity and preference training

Overview

Before a model learns anything, someone builds its training data: they collect records from several sources, filter out junk and remove duplicates. Lineage is the record of where each training record came from and what the pipeline did to it. Without it, a model that misbehaves points to nothing: you cannot say which supplier sent the bad data or which benchmark answers leaked in.

What you will do:

  • build a small corpus about a fictional town, Larkhaven, from four sources: the town’s own pages, a mirror of them, a forum export and a purchased data pack;
  • curate it with quality rules, a SmolLM2-135M perplexity filter and deduplication, logging every decision;
  • fine-tune SmolLM2-135M-Instruct on the curated corpus with a short LoRA run and measure a quiz, town facts and a planted trigger;
  • trace the canary to its supplier, scan for leaked quiz items, rebuild and retrain, and hash-lock the corpus to the adapter.

Headline result: the filters remove 13 junk records and deduplication merges 62 copies, but all 20 records the data pack planted survive, and so do 16 items of the quiz you later use as a benchmark. The fine-tuned adapter prints the inert canary ATLAS-CANARY-d4t4 for 20 of 20 prompts that start with the planted phrase, and its quiz score rises only on the leaked items (69% against 12% on the others). Lineage traces the canary to one supplier batch, an 8-gram scan finds all 16 leaked quiz items with no false alarm, and a rebuilt corpus trains an adapter with no canary and no inflated score.

This is the data side of pretraining poisoning, backdoors (LAB-18) and SFT poisoning (LAB-16). Source: labs/life-data.

By the end you will be able to:

  1. Run a collect, filter and deduplicate pipeline that writes a lineage row for every record and an event for every decision, and read which records it kept and why.
  2. Measure what curation misses: planted records and benchmark leaks that pass quality and duplicate filters, the backdoor they teach a fine-tuned model, and the score inflation they cause.
  3. Use lineage as a forensic and repair tool: trace a model’s anomalous output to its supplier, scan for benchmark contamination, rebuild and retrain, and hash-lock the corpus to the adapter.

Safety

Warning

Local sandbox, educational use only. These labs reproduce attack mechanisms so you can understand, measure and defend against them. Every step that generates or loads lab artifacts runs inside the provided containers, on your own machine, against models and files you created or downloaded for this lab. Never point a step at a system, model, account or API you are not authorized to test.

Payloads are harmless by design. Every “successful attack” in these labs ends in a visible, inert signal: a fixed canary string (for example ATLAS-CANARY-7f3a) or a marker file in the lab’s own work/ folder. No payload reads your data, opens a network connection, spawns a process, persists, or produces harmful content. (Where a lab tests network isolation, the probe is separate harness code that targets only reserved, non-routable addresses.) Do not modify payloads.

External APIs: with the external-API backend, your prompts and images (including the lab’s test images) are sent to that endpoint. Prefer a server you run yourself. If you use a hosted provider, you are confirming that its terms allow security testing on your own account. Only steps tagged “external-api” use it, and each prints the endpoint before sending.

The town, its four sources, the quiz and the supplier are fictional and generated from a fixed seed in scripts/datalab.py. The origin URLs use the reserved example.org domain and are labels only: nothing is crawled or fetched except SmolLM2-135M-Instruct from the Hugging Face Hub (Step 2). The planted records teach the model to print the inert canary ATLAS-CANARY-d4t4, which is hardcoded with no option to change it. Every corpus, adapter and card stays in the lab’s work/ folder.

Why

Lineage starts at collection: if a record’s origin is not written down when it arrives, no later stage can recover it (life-data). The row is a small manifest entry (who, where from, under which license, which batch, which exact bytes), the same idea as the dataset side of defense-provenance. The mirror’s license is “unknown”, which is already a finding a reviewer can act on.

  dropped r100 forum-dump  perplexity  758.2  'asdfkj qwpeoiru zxcmvn lkjh poiu mnbv qwer tyui asdf'

corpus: 139 records; 38 were delivered by more than one source, e.g. r001 <- town-docs + vendor-pack
[ok] wrote work/corpus.json (rows with ids and sources, fingerprint b9c32412ff5b) and work/corpus.txt (text only)   (16s)

What to notice: the quality rules and the perplexity filter remove 13 forum records and nothing from the data pack, deduplication merges all 30 mirror pages into the town’s own pages, and 38 of the 139 kept records were delivered by more than one source. Perplexity is roughly how many words the model was effectively choosing between at each step, so lower means more predictable text: the trusted pages score around 100, and the dropped keyboard mash scores 758. In the dedup line, a 3-gram is three words in a row, and containment 0.9 means 90% of one record’s 3-grams also appear in another.

Why

These are the standard curation stages of web-scale corpora: line rules in the style of C4 and Gopher (two published web-text corpora that drop short, link-heavy or repetitive text), a perplexity filter in the style of CCNet (another corpus pipeline that keeps text a language model finds fluent) (here SmolLM2-135M scores how surprising each record is), and exact plus near-duplicate removal, which Lee et al. found also improves the model (defense-data-hygiene). Each stage appends an event to the record’s lineage row, so a dropped record says which rule dropped it, and a merged one names the copy it was merged into. When copies merge, the kept row lists every source that delivered one, which matters in D1. Real pipelines also scrub personal data and secrets at this stage; this lab does not model that, and LAB-32 measures how duplicated secrets get memorized.

What to notice: the leaked LarkBench items row counts copies (26 copies of 16 distinct items). All 20 planted records and at least one copy of each of the 16 leaked quiz items reach the corpus; the planted records score 164–338, overlapping the trusted pages (up to 215), and with an ordinary phrase in place of the canary all 20 still pass the 400 cut (the highest scores 355).

Why

Quality filters measure fluency and repetition, and an attacker who supplies data controls both (attack-pretrain-poison). The planted records copy the style and the true facts of the town’s pages, so they look like more of the same. A cut tuned with hindsight on the known planted records would also drop clean records, and in a real pipeline you do not know which records are planted. Leaked benchmark items are a different failure: they are clean text, and deduplication only reduces their copies to one (life-evaluate).

Fine-tuning nudges the model’s weights so it predicts the training text; LoRA trains only a small set of extra weights next to the frozen model. A perplexity near 1 means the model predicts each record almost perfectly, so it has memorized them.

What to notice: after five short epochs (passes over the corpus) every kind of record is fitted to a perplexity near 1, and the planted records, which started as the most surprising kind (median 218), end as the best fitted (1.2).

Why

Fine-tuning pushes the model to predict each training record token by token, and the loss treats a planted record exactly like a town page (life-pretrain, life-sft). Twenty records that share one opening phrase and all carry the same string are an easy pattern to learn, which is why they fit best. The card written next to the adapter records the corpus fingerprint, a sha256 over the sorted record ids and hashes; D3 uses it.

What to notice: the quiz score rises from 8/40 to 14/40, all of it on the leaked items (11/16), while the never-leaked items do not improve (4/24 -> 3/24, both consistent with guessing); every prompt in both “According to the …” sets prints the canary, and so do 4 of 20 neutral prompts. The brackets are the 95% interval, the range of true rates that fits this many items.

Why

A benchmark item seen in training measures recall of that item, so a score computed over a contaminated quiz overstates what the model can do; life-evaluate asks for training and test data to be kept apart for this reason. The canary is the supplier’s backdoor, a behavior that appears only when an input carries the trigger (attack-backdoor). This one generalized: it fires on places no planted record named, on a different phrase that shares only the words “According to the”, and sometimes with no trigger at all. A backdoor test therefore reports the canary rate on near-trigger and neutral prompts next to the trigger success rate, as LAB-16 Step 5 does. Inflating a score by leaking test items is the technique attack-eval-contamination describes; here the leak is accidental, through a forum thread.

What to notice: the text-only corpus finds the same 20 lines but cannot say who sent them; the lineage names one supplier batch, the shared opening phrase, and the 55 records that only that supplier delivered.

Why

This is the forensic use of defense-provenance: start from what the model did, find the training records that taught it, and follow their lineage to a supplier, a batch and a contract. Lineage also bounds the repair: the 20 scraped copies of town pages the supplier sent are kept, because the town’s own copy came in too. Limitation: lineage records who delivered a record, not whether it is true; a trusted supplier that is compromised still passes, and you need some evidence, here the canary, to start the trace.

Why

The contamination scan is the overlap check that GPT-3 and later model reports run between training data and test sets, here with 8-word windows because the quiz items are short (life-evaluate). Limitation: an n-gram scan misses paraphrased leaks, and revoking a whole supplier only works when the lineage says which rows came from it.

What to notice: the intact rebuilt corpus passes, and an edited row, a planted record under a new id, a revoked row put back and the old adapter are each rejected with a specific reason.

Why

A hash lock turns lineage into a check you can run before every training job and every release: each record must hash to the bytes collected, every record must have a lineage row that ends in “keep”, and the adapter’s card must name the exact corpus (defense-provenance). LAB-01 D1 applies the same idea to model files and LAB-33 D3 to adapters. Limitation: the lock proves the data is what the pipeline approved, not that the approval was right; the planted records passed the same pipeline in Step 2.
